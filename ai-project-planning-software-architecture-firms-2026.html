AI software automates project planning at the seams.

AI software can automate parts of project planning for architecture firms, chiefly budget setup, staffing suggestions, data queries and risk alerts. Monograph is the clearest small-firm fit, Mosaic concentrates on resources, and Deltek Vantagepoint suits firms already running a deeper ERP. None writes an accountable project plan for you.

Which AI software automates project planning for architecture firms?

Monograph automates budget setup and staffing suggestions, Mosaic focuses on resource allocation and project monitoring, and Deltek Vantagepoint adds AI risk insights to a full A&E ERP. No credible product automates the whole plan. Choose the one that removes your firm's worst planning bottleneck while leaving scope, sequence and approval with a project manager.

A project manager receives a signed agreement at 4:48 on Friday. By Monday morning it must become phases, fee allocations, staff hours and a schedule everyone can misunderstand in a slightly different way. This is the planning seam where current AI earns attention.

It does not earn the phrase autopilot. The credible products automate narrow acts around a plan: reading a contract, suggesting staff, surfacing a budget variance, or finding an answer buried in project data. The project manager still decides what the work means. That boundary is less cinematic than a bot drawing a Gantt chart from thin air, and much more useful.

Monograph is the most direct answer to the search query. Its current product material says its AI Budget Builder can create project budgets from contracts, while AI staffing recommendations use project experience and capacity. The same Monograph page says schedule changes can cascade through linked phases and its AR assistant handles overdue-invoice follow-up. Those are several automations around a project, not one machine-generated master plan.

The distinction matters. Monograph's official Project Planner page describes phase budgets, staff assignments, consultant allowances and schedule planning. A project manager supplies the structure and checks the result. AI can reduce the blank cells. It cannot decide that entitlement risk deserves three extra weeks or that a difficult client needs an earlier drawing review.

Monograph is the cleanest start for architecture firms

Monograph was built around architecture and engineering practice rather than adapted from generic task management. Its project object connects phases, fees, staffing, time and invoices. That common data model is the real advantage. An AI feature can only make a sensible staffing suggestion if staff capacity and project phases already live in the same system.

The product's current site says Ask Monograph answers questions about project status, time, utilization and profitability from firm data. It also says QuickBooks Online integration covers invoices, bills, expenses, clients and consultants in the United States, but timesheets and payroll do not sync. That integration limit is stated in Monograph's own FAQ, and it is exactly the sort of dull sentence that should outrank a sparkling AI demo. A missing handoff becomes manual work every week.

ArchiGen's take: shortlist Monograph when the firm's current plan is a spreadsheet stitched to timesheets and accounting by memory. Do not buy it because a contract can become a draft budget. Buy it if the resulting plan, actual hours and billing record stay connected after the novelty has worn off.

Mosaic treats staffing as the planning problem

Mosaic starts from a different diagnosis. The hard part is not making the phase list; it is assigning the right people across many moving projects without burning them out or leaving expensive gaps. Its architecture page says the platform can pull people, projects, time and budgets from existing systems, then use resource planning and forecasting across that combined data. Mosaic also claims AI monitoring can flag scope creep. Those are Mosaic's own product claims for architecture firms, not an independent performance test.

That makes Mosaic attractive when the firm already has accounting and project systems it does not intend to replace. It sits above them as a resource-planning layer. The trial should therefore begin with integration, not recommendation quality. If project stages, leave, skills or budget changes arrive late, the clever staffing screen will be confidently behind.

Ask one concrete question during a pilot: can the platform show who can cover a six-week technical-design phase starting next month, with the required experience, without breaking another committed plan? Then inspect the source fields behind the answer. A suggestion without current capacity data is office astrology with nicer typography.

When does Deltek Vantagepoint make more sense?

Deltek Vantagepoint is the heavyweight option. It joins business development, resource planning, project delivery, accounting and billing in one A&E-oriented ERP. Deltek says its Dela layer can generate summaries, answer natural-language questions, automate routine tasks and surface risks. The useful 2026 detail is more precise: Dela runs nightly project analyses and surfaces three critical issues, focused on differences among earned, invoiced and spent metrics, with recommended actions.

Deltek's documentation also says a project plan is created automatically when a project is added. Managers can then assign labor, expenses and consultants, schedule work-breakdown elements and plan pursuits before the contract is won. The official Vantagepoint help page makes clear that “automatic” creates the plan record. It does not invent the delivery strategy.

Vantagepoint earns a look when project planning is inseparable from accounting, pipeline and compliance, and when the firm can support ERP implementation. It is a poor answer to a small firm's request for a clever scheduler if the surrounding system is more machinery than the practice needs. Software can remove admin and still create administration.

Do you need AI, or merely connected planning?

BQE CORE is a useful control case. BQE's architecture material describes connected project management, time, accounting, billing, CRM and resource planning. Its project-planning page promises workload, capacity, project and historical-data visibility. The page does not substantiate a feature that authors an architecture project plan with AI.

That is not a mark against it. A firm whose real problem is duplicate entry may gain more from connected data and ordinary workflow automation than from a language model. The AI label is not the outcome. Fewer stale plans, earlier budget warnings and less time rebuilding the same staffing table are outcomes.

This is the same discipline behind ArchiGen's argument to count automated tasks, not AI projects. Name the manual action and its frequency. If nobody can describe the action, the purchase brief is still a mood board.

How should an architecture firm run the pilot?

Use one completed project and one active project. Rebuild the completed plan from its agreement, phases, staffing and actuals. Then connect the active plan and change one deadline, one staff absence and one fee assumption. Record which dependent fields update, which alerts appear and which edits still require a person.

Score five things: setup hours, data freshness, explainability, correction time and export quality. Ask the vendor to show the source behind every AI suggestion. For staffing, that means availability, skills and project experience. For a risk flag, it means the actual budget, earned-value or invoice variance. For a budget draft, it means the contract clause and the phase mapping.

Data governance belongs in the pilot too. The American Institute of Architects tells firms to examine storage and training practices, avoid putting confidential client or firm data into unvetted systems, and retain qualified professional accountability. Those are explicit points in the AIA's responsible-AI guidance, last updated 22 October 2025. Our guide to client confidentiality in cloud AI systems provides a practical vendor-question set.

Finally, make a second project manager reproduce the workflow. If the automation depends on one operations lead knowing which settings are sacred, the firm has not removed a bottleneck. It has given the bottleneck a dashboard.

Which product belongs on the shortlist?

Start with Monograph if the practice wants an A&E-specific operating system and needs budget, staffing and project-data assistance without a large ERP programme. Start with Mosaic if resource allocation across teams is the central pain and existing systems will remain. Start with Vantagepoint if the firm already needs deep project accounting and wants AI monitoring inside that record.

Then apply the same procurement rule used for rendering platforms: test the failure mode. Our AI platform selection guide focuses on revision loops, data terms and measurable correction cost. Here, the equivalent test is a late phase, an absent team member and a fee variance. See what the software notices. See what it quietly leaves for Monday morning.

AI can draft the cells and watch the numbers. The project manager still owns the promise.
