Tool Review · 10 May 2026 · 7 min read

AI render tools for architecture and interior design.

Six AI renderers now pitch to architects and interior designers at once: Spacely, Rendair, mnml.ai, Fenestra, Interior AI and Chaos Veras. Entry prices run from $8 to $49 a month on the vendors' pages or blogs. This guide reads their pricing pages and documentation. ArchiGen has not run any of them.

Which AI render tools cover both architecture and interior design?

Spacely, Rendair, mnml.ai and Fenestra each sell interior and exterior rendering from sketches, photos or model views. Interior AI is interior-first. Chaos Veras renders from a live model inside Revit, SketchUp, Rhino and Archicad. Choose by what you start from: a room photo, a sketch, or a model you are still editing.

This page used to report a week of kitchen, hotel-lobby and dental-office tests through Spacely. ArchiGen ran none of them. The tests are gone, along with a star rating, a "15 percent material drift" figure and a Spacely price list that no longer matches Spacely's. What is left is a documentation review: every claim below comes from a vendor page or press report opened on 28 October 2026.

The earlier version also framed interiors as the place exterior-first renderers fail. That may be true. Nobody here measured it, so it goes too.

What does Spacely AI actually do?

The Spacely homepage sells "Instant AI Interior Design & Room Rendering" and promises to "Turn a sketch, 3D model, or room photo into a photorealistic render in under 60 seconds." It claims more than 440,000 users across architects, interior designers, realtors and owners. The company is a Bangkok startup; The AI Insider reported a $1 million seed round in July 2025, led by PropTech Farm Fund III, under CEO Paruey Anadirekkul.

Despite the interior branding, the tools page covers both sides of the wall. Creative Interior and Creative Exterior turn "sketches or clay models" into styled renders. Auto Furnish fills an empty room "without altering the layout"; Empty the Room clears one. Style Transfer copies a mood board, Image to 3D builds a textured model from one picture, and Image to Video makes "an 8-second architectural video from one or two frames." The virtual staging page offers living room, bedroom, kitchen and office as room types. The old article's clinical and dental room types do not appear on any Spacely page opened for this rewrite, and neither does its "named material catalog"; the pricing page lists "Material settings."

Host software is where the pages disagree with each other. A SketchUp extension exists and renders "right inside SketchUp." The pricing page lists "SketchUp, Revit, AutoCAD, Rhino" as integrations on paid plans, but Spacely's own June 2026 pricing post calls Revit, Rhino and AutoCAD integrations "arriving soon." Assume SketchUp until the others appear in a changelog.

Photo, sketch or live model: where does your job start?

The vendor pages sort themselves neatly once you ask what goes in. If the starting point is a photo of an existing room, the staging tools are built for it: Spacely's Auto Furnish and Empty the Room, Rendair's photo swaps, Interior AI's room types. That is renovation and real-estate work, and the pricing reflects it.

If the starting point is a model that is still changing, the plugin tools matter more. Veras renders from the open model in the host, and mnml.ai ships free plugins for four hosts. Fenestra and Rendair take an exported file or a screenshot instead, which is fine until the design moves and the export goes stale. Our note on native integration versus PNG round-trips sets out what that costs in a review cycle, and Veras against Rendair puts the two approaches side by side.

For interiors you want to control node by node, the local route is ComfyUI, with its own problems; our piece on ComfyUI for interiors explains why exterior graphs break indoors.

What do none of these pages promise?

Specification accuracy. None of the product or pricing pages opened for this article says its renders will match a finish schedule, a millwork drawing or a lighting calculation. They sell images. That is a perfectly good thing to sell, as long as nobody downstream treats the picture as the spec.

Terms deserve a read before client work. Spacely's terms page did not load when checked, so its commercial-licence position rests on the pricing-page bullet alone. Interior AI's no-refund line is worth knowing before a yearly plan. If you want a structured way to spend trial credits, our trial-credit evaluation plan is built for exactly this shortlist.

Six vendors, one category, and the honest differentiator is the input box.
