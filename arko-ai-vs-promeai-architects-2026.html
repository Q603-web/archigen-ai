Arko AI vs PromeAI: Which Fits Your Workflow?

Arko AI is worth considering for rendering from a supported model viewport; PromeAI is the stronger shortlist candidate for sketch and image editing workflows. That is a recommendation based on their documented features, not an image-quality ranking. Check Arko’s published host versions before paying.

Should architects choose Arko AI or PromeAI?

Shortlist Arko AI if you want to start from a supported modeling application, and PromeAI if your work starts with sketches, screenshots or image edits. Their public documentation supports those different entry points. It does not establish a winner for geometric accuracy, render quality or time saved on your projects.

A window that moves is a design change, even when the brickwork gets prettier. That is the useful starting point for this comparison. Decide what the renderer may reinterpret before comparing its images. A loose material study and a view of an approved facade need different levels of freedom.

Arko and PromeAI deserve a comparison built around that distinction. The earlier version of this page described a shared project trial that did not happen. This rewrite replaces those claims with opened product documentation and editorial selection criteria. No retained score, timing or image is evidence of an ArchiGen trial.

Arko starts at the model window

Arko’s home page lists Rhino, Revit and SketchUp as host applications. Its Rhino page describes rendering the active view, with discipline and style choices. That makes Arko a candidate when you already have a model and want to explore an image from a particular camera. The starting point is concrete: your chosen view.

Compatibility needs a closer look. The Rhino page explicitly names Rhino 7 and Windows. Those pages do not establish support for every current installation. If your office depends on another version, request confirmation for that exact setup before subscribing. An application logo is a poor substitute for a version number.

The Rhino page describes its Basic model as suitable for ideation and promotes its Pro model for geometry retention. Those are vendor descriptions. They establish how Arko positions the choices, not a measured accuracy difference. Ask a trial image to preserve the awkward parts of your design: a recessed entrance, a thin canopy or an uneven window rhythm. These are proposed checks, not reported Arko failures.

PromeAI starts with a broader image brief

PromeAI’s product page describes Sketch Rendering from sketches, photographs and modeling screenshots. It also lists region editing, Erase & Replace and outpainting. That documented combination makes it a more natural candidate when the work includes both an initial concept image and subsequent local revisions.

The Sketch Rendering page describes prompts, style choices and optional reference images. It also lists OBJ, FBX, STL and 3DS uploads. File acceptance alone says nothing about whether the resulting workflow preserves object identities or produces editable BIM elements. Ask to see the output you actually need, rather than assuming that importing a model makes the whole process model-based.

For a sketch-led commission, start by deciding what the drawing leaves unresolved. A blank patch could invite a material choice; a carefully drawn opening should not. Give a reference image a specific job, such as finish or atmosphere, and keep it out of decisions about layout. This is a suggested evaluation method for either product, not a claim that either will obey every instruction.

Price the revision, not the first picture

Arko’s pricing page describes monthly render quotas, recurring subscriptions and additional render credits that require an active subscription. Within its subscription Pro Toolset, it identifies Seed Settings and Negative Prompts as controls for the Basic model. Read the currently selected plan before treating an old feature list as a promise about every engine.

This comparison does not repeat an unverified dollar price or call either tool cheaper. Instead, estimate what you need to deliver. Count alternative images, local revisions and final exports separately. If you need a whole presentation set, make the evaluation include that set. A low entry price is only useful if the relevant allowance covers the work.

For PromeAI, confirm the plan’s current generation allowance, output resolution and commercial-use terms before making a purchase. The product pages cited here describe tools; they are not a substitute for the agreement attached to your account. Treat the site’s “Start Creating For Free” button as an invitation to inspect the terms, not an assumption about unlimited production.

What would a useful comparison look like?

Use one representative design with a clear camera and an annotated source image. Mark the elements that must stay: the number of openings, roof silhouette and ground contact. Then allow a defined change, perhaps replacing pale render with brick. Save every result, including the rejected ones, and note any repair needed before an image could appear on a board.

Next ask for one small revision to the accepted image. Keep the brief narrow enough that a reviewer can see whether it happened. If the request is “change the cladding,” an altered entrance is not a bonus. Evaluate the effort of reaching an acceptable revision, including retries and manual correction, rather than choosing a winner from two attractive first attempts.

For a more formal record, use the categories in our geometry review checklist. For the buying decision, the cost-per-usable-image guide gives a better question than the subscription headline. Neither link supplies missing test results for Arko or PromeAI.

The sensible shortlist

Choose an Arko trial around the model and host you already have. Choose a PromeAI trial around the images you need to create and revise. If both fit, compare them on the same deliverable with clearly recorded input differences. There is no reason to award a universal crown before either has earned a place in that particular workflow.

Keep the first evaluation small enough to repeat after an update. Save the source camera, image dimensions and prompt alongside your notes. Record the date and the selected model where the interface identifies it. That gives a future comparison a starting point and prevents a remembered good image from becoming an unsupported claim about the product as a whole.
