Autodesk Forma for architects: what the AI actually does

Autodesk Forma's AI mostly predicts wind and noise in seconds, alongside sun, daylight and carbon analyses, while its generative layout tools are still early. For site feasibility and massing it is worth opening, and most Revit and AEC Collection subscribers already have it. This review uses Autodesk's pages and independent reporting; ArchiGen has not run Forma.

What does Autodesk Forma do for architects?

Autodesk Forma is a browser-based family of early-design tools. Forma Site Design covers site context, massing and environmental analysis: sun, daylight, wind, noise, microclimate and carbon. Forma Building Design, launched in April 2026, adds facades, floor plans and units. Both send geolocated models to Revit, and both come with Revit and AEC Collection subscriptions.

Somewhere in your Autodesk account there is probably a Forma licence you never asked for. Since April 2026, Autodesk says, every Revit subscriber has had access to Forma Site Design, Forma Building Design, Forma Board and Forma Data Management Essentials, and the AEC Collection includes Forma too. So the real question is whether it deserves a morning, and what the AI in the pitch actually is.

This is a documentation review: ArchiGen has not run Forma, so nothing below judges its results. Vendor claims are labelled as vendor claims.

Which Forma is which?

Forma has been renamed often enough to need a family tree. Autodesk released the original Forma on 8 May 2023, combining what its product FAQ calls "many of Spacemaker's AI and predictive analytics capabilities" with conceptual modelling tools from FormIt. Spacemaker, which Autodesk bought in 2020, was then retired. At Autodesk University in September 2025, AEC Magazine reported that the product would become Forma Site Design, while "Autodesk Forma" now names Autodesk's whole industry cloud, which also covers data management (Forma Data Management, formerly Autodesk Docs) and construction tools.

For an architect, three names matter. Forma Site Design is the site planning and analysis tool. Forma Building Design, launched on 7 April 2026 as what Autodesk's launch post calls "the first step", handles schematic design: facades, floor plans, units and unit mix. Autodesk says it is built "especially" for "larger-scale residential and mixed-use urban projects with repetition across facade elements and floor plan layouts." AEC Magazine's Greg Corke described its target detail as LOD 200. Forma for Buildings is the bundle of both, plus Forma Data Management and Forma Board, a shared review canvas.

What AI features does Forma have?

Mostly, fast physics. Autodesk's August 2026 explainer lists analyses for sun hours, shadow, daylight potential, wind, noise, views, solar energy potential, microclimate and total carbon. Daylight potential is scored with Sky Component values. Total carbon, which covers embodied, operational and total carbon through Forma Carbon Insights, is marked on the product page as available for US hubs only.

Two analyses come at two speeds, and that split is the honest heart of Forma's AI. Autodesk's wind tutorial says rapid wind "runs instantly", that its results "are based on AI model predictions and are more directional than the simulation-based detailed analysis", and that it is "best used during active design." The detailed version is computational fluid dynamics, and Autodesk says it "can be used for documentation and final results." A reply in a 2023 Autodesk Community thread (a community source, and an old one) said it is "normal for this analysis to take around 1 hour." Noise works the same way.

Read that as a machine-learning shortcut to the slow answer: good enough to flag that the north tower is probably chilling the plaza, not, by Autodesk's own framing, the thing you put in a planning report. A coloured site plan is not a daylight study just because it looks like one.

Is there a "Forma gen"?

Not under that name: ArchiGen found no Autodesk product called Forma gen. The generative features that exist are two. Site Automation, formerly called Explore, is what an April 2025 Autodesk post calls "the latest release of our generative design feature." It generates building and circulation layouts inside a site boundary from grid, setback and typology inputs, updating area metrics as you go. The same post warns that converting generated buildings for editing in 3D Sketch "is not reversible."

The newer piece is Building Layout Explorer, released on 2 June 2026 as an experimental feature for commercial Site Design users with data stored in the US. Autodesk says it generates floor plan options from a massing model using generative AI "trained on aggregated 3D AEC data", informed by massing, building type and structural material, as part of its "neural CAD" work. Autodesk concedes that "some outputs will be more useful than others." At AU 2026 in September, Autodesk said it can compare multifamily layouts against a client's unit mix, and that Autodesk Assistant in Site Design "will be able to answer natural-language questions about a project site." Future tense, note.

How does Forma connect to Revit, Rhino and Dynamo?

Revit is the main road out. Autodesk's explainer says that from Revit 2027, Revit is a "Forma Connected Client", reading and writing the same data in Forma Data Management; for Revit 2025 and 2026, an add-in converts a Site Design project into a native, geolocated Revit model. Building Design exports a native Revit model "complete with site context and families for windows, slabs, and walls." AEC Magazine reported in April 2026 that the Connected Client had launched as a Tech Preview, with some Forma capabilities, such as environmental analysis, runnable inside Revit. The BIM-first AI stack covers what else lives in Revit.

Rhino users get the Rhino to Forma extension, which, according to Autodesk's 2023 announcement, syncs geometry from Rhino into Forma for analysis and sends Forma terrain and surrounding buildings back. Dynamo arrives as the Dynamo Player extension. Its cloud version, Dynamo as a service, creates native Forma elements with nothing to install, according to the Dynamo team's 2024 post, and the Dynamo primer lists it as an open beta for all Forma users. Forma also imports IFC and OBJ and hosts third-party extensions; AEC Magazine names Finch, TestFit, ShapeDiver and Chaos Veras among them.

How much does Forma cost?

Autodesk's US list prices on 10 October 2026:

Route Forma tools included US list price
Forma Site Design Site Design $85/month, $700/year, $2,100 for 3 years
Forma for Buildings Site Design, Building Design, Data Management, Board $125/month, $1,000/year, $2,995 for 3 years
Revit subscription Site Design, Building Design, Board, Data Management Essentials Included; Revit price not shown on the Forma pages
AEC Collection Site Design and Building Design, plus Revit, AutoCAD and other products $460/month, $3,675/year, $11,030 for 3 years

Building Design has no standalone price; the Building Design page lists only the AEC Collection, Revit and the Forma for Buildings bundle. The Site Design page's headline "Starting at $59 / month" sits close to the $700 annual price spread over twelve months. Flex tokens and a free trial are also offered. Watch one cost off the price list: AEC Magazine reported in 2025 that cloud participation runs on monitored API calls with "generous monthly limits", and that some customers worried about costs once those limits are reached.

Where does Forma stop?

At the drawing set. Autodesk's own pages position Building Design for exploration and Revit "for detailed design development and documentation", and AEC Magazine reported in 2025 that Building Design is intended to deliver models at LOD 200.

Geography is the quieter limit. Total carbon is US-only for now, Building Layout Explorer needs data stored in the US, and the Site Design FAQ says automated context data "vary" from country to country. Lisbon and Phoenix are not buying the same product. Fit matters too: by Autodesk's description, Building Design pays off where facades and floor plans repeat, which reads as a poor match for a one-off house or an interior.

AEC Magazine framed Building Design against "BIM 2.0" startups including Motif, Arcol, Snaptrude and Qonic. If you are weighing them, the Snaptrude review sets out a comparable Revit round trip, and it helps to compare tools that do the same job before ranking them.

Is Forma replacing Revit?

Autodesk's answer, at AU in September 2026, is "Over time, yes. More and more of the work that happens in Revit today will happen directly in Forma," though "We will not force a sudden transition." Architosh noted that essentially all of Autodesk's cloud applications now carry Forma names. The shift worth noticing is cultural more than technical. Early design used to end in a file you owned and emailed. In the Connected Client model it ends in shared data on Autodesk's cloud, which is convenient right up to the day you want to leave.

This article cannot say how close rapid wind lands to the detailed simulation, how useful Building Layout Explorer's plans are, or how clean the Revit export is on your templates. The licence you may already have is the place to find out. Open it for the cheap early questions: where the shadow falls, where the wind bites, how many units fit. Keep the answers that carry a signature for the slow simulation and the specialist.

Forma is a very fast sketch of the truth. Use it like one.
