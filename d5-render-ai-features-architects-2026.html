D5 Render AI in 2026: what each feature actually does.

D5 Render now claims more than fifteen built-in AI features, and almost none of them are called what the internet thinks they are. This is a release-note reading of D5 Render 3.0 and 3.1, its pricing and its hardware limits, with every feature named the way D5 names it. ArchiGen has not run D5 on a project.

What AI features does D5 Render have in 2026?

D5 Render says version 3.0 carries "15+ built-in AI features." The headline ones are AI Scene Match, AI Asset Recommendation and AI Image to 3D, added in January 2026, alongside AI PBR Material Snap, AI Enhancer, AI Style Transfer and AI Inpainting. The free Community plan gets 50 trial AI credits; Pro and Teams list unlimited.

An earlier version of this page described a week of project work in D5 that ArchiGen never did, and three AI features D5 does not sell under those names: an "AI Generator," an "AI lighting" tool and an AI-tagged asset search. They are gone. What replaces them is less dramatic and more useful: the features as D5's own release notes list them, checked on 28 October 2026.

Where D5 is in 2026

Two releases matter this year. D5 Render 3.0 landed on 7 January 2026 with the new AI Agent tools, oceans, volumetric clouds and Gaussian splat import. D5 Render 3.1 followed on 8 July, with a 3.1.1 hotfix on 30 July, the latest version listed when this page was updated. D5's 3.0 launch post is where the "15+ built-in AI features" line comes from. Count the release notes back to 2.10 and you can get close to that number, as long as you count generously.

Underneath the AI sits the thing D5 has always sold: a real-time renderer with path tracing, which its pricing guide lists on every plan, including the free one. The denoising that makes low-sample path tracing look finished is, in D5's words, an "RTX-accelerated AI denoiser" that "handles both real-time previews and final exports." D5 does not give it a product name or a settings page of its own. It is plumbing, and D5 barely talks about it.

The AI Agent: scene, assets, objects

Version 3.0 bundled three tools under the AI Agent label. They are the closest D5 gets to the "AI lighting" and "AI Generator" ideas, and they work on the scene rather than on a flat screenshot.

AI Scene Match

Type an atmosphere ("Autumn dusk" and "Snowy winter" are D5's own examples) and the Agent generates reference images, then, per the launch post, "with a single click, it matches the lighting and post-processing parameters" to your scene. The release notes describe it as "intelligent lighting and environment setup for models based on intent descriptions." That is a lighting first draft driven by a sentence, applied to your actual geometry. Whether the draft is any good on a particular building is something only your own scene will tell you.

AI Asset Recommendation

The same reference image, or a text prompt, can be used to pull suggested models from D5's asset library. Architosh's report on 3.0 frames it as a time saver for populating scenes. This is the real version of the "semantic asset search" the old page invented: recommendation from an image or prompt, not a rebuilt search box.

AI Image to 3D

Upload up to three reference photos of an object from different angles and D5 reconstructs a 3D model from them. D5 calls the result "high-accuracy" and promises it "in seconds." It sits beside an older Text to 3D tool that D5's AI page says is "powered by Meshy." For set dressing, useful. For anything a contractor will price, model it.

Materials, and the image tools

AI PBR Material Snap arrived in D5 Render 2.11 (July 2025): upload a reference image up to 6K, select an area, and D5 generates PBR textures from it. Version 3.0 claims better matching accuracy and more variation in results, and 3.1 added aspect-ratio presets (1:1, 16:9, 3:4), tile direction and a high-definition quality option. Of everything on this list, it is the feature most likely to save a materials person real time, because the output is a texture you can still edit.

The image-side tools are older. D5 Render 2.10 (March 2025) added AI Inpainting, which D5 says "fills missing elements like sky, vegetation, or water automatically," and improved AI Enhancer and AI Style Transfer. Version 2.11 brought AI Atmosphere Match 2.0, which suggests style-matched references and adds an AI Intensity control. Version 3.0 says AI Enhancer now gives "sharper image definition" tuned for interiors. These work on rendered images, so they carry the usual risk of any image-to-image pass: the picture can drift from the model. Our note on native integration versus PNG upload covers why that matters for anything a client will hold you to.

2.11 also introduced an earlier AI Agent with SmartPlanting, which lays out gardens in three styles, a Plant Schedule export of species, sizes and quantities, and D5 Bot, a help chat. The plant schedule is the quietly practical one for planting coordination.

What 3.1 added, mostly not AI

The July release is a reminder that D5's best work in 2026 is not branded AI. Per the 3.1 notes, it added Interactive Presentation mode for switching scene states with clickable triggers, Seasonal Vegetation, an ACEScg working colour space, stereoscopic panoramas, HeightField Tracing for displacement, path tracing for Gaussian splat assets, and super resolution upgraded to DLSS 4.5. It also put D5 Lite into Rhino and Revit as betas. Architosh read 3.1 as a turn toward live design review rather than finished stills, which is a fair summary of the list.

How much is D5 Render, and what do AI credits cost?

D5's pricing page sorts AI by credits. As listed on 28 October 2026, Community is free with "50 trial AI credits," 2,100-plus assets and limited tours; Pro and Teams both list unlimited AI credits, with 10 GB and 100 GB of cloud storage respectively. The pricing page loads its prices in the browser, so the figures below come from D5's own pricing guide, also opened on 28 October 2026.

Fifty credits is a taster, not a plan. The free tier is honest about the rendering, and the AI is the upsell.

Hardware, plugins and the Mac question

D5 Render is Windows software. Its system requirements ask for Windows 10 v1809 or later and an NVIDIA GTX 1060 6GB, AMD Radeon RX 6400 or Intel Arc A3 or better. Architosh notes there is no native Mac version because the engine is built on DirectX 12. The download page lists LiveSync or sync plugins for SketchUp, 3ds Max, Rhino, Archicad, Revit, Cinema 4D, Blender and Vectorworks, which covers most of the modellers a practice is likely to own.

Who should care

If you already render in D5, the 3.0 Agent tools and the 3.1 Material Snap controls are worth a look on your own scenes, because they act on the model, not on a screenshot. If you are shopping for an AI renderer that restyles a design from a view, D5's image tools exist, but its pitch is a real-time engine with AI around the edges. For tools built the other way round, see our AI architecture rendering roundup and the Twinmotion workflow piece. Mac studios can stop reading here.

D5's AI is a toolbox bolted to a fast engine. The engine is still the reason to buy it.
