Enscape's AI Material Generator and Enhancer, by the credit

Enscape's AI Material Generator turns a photo into a PBR material for 5 to 20 Chaos Credits, and the Chaos AI Enhancer refines the people and vegetation in a finished render for 10 to 20. Both are worth using as finishing tools on background work, and both share one monthly credit allowance with Veras.

What do Enscape's AI Material Generator and AI Enhancer do?

The AI Material Generator, added as a beta in Enscape 4.14, turns an uploaded photo or texture into an Enscape material with albedo, bump/normal and roughness maps. The Chaos AI Enhancer, introduced with Enscape 4.1, sends a render to Chaos Cloud and refines its people, vegetation and large surfaces. Both spend Chaos Credits.

ArchiGen has not run either feature. This explainer is built from Chaos's own release notes, documentation, blog posts and pricing pages, a Chaos reseller's credit table, a Chaos forum thread and independent coverage from AEC Magazine, Architosh and Digital Production, all checked on 10 October 2026. Where a capability is Chaos's description, it is labelled as Chaos's.

The most useful sentence in the whole pile is a modest one. Chaos's December 2025 launch post calls the AI Material Generator "ideal for secondary materials that don't require art direction." A vendor steering you, in its own launch copy, away from the stone your client chose is rare candour, and the right frame for all of Enscape's AI: finishing tools, billed per use.

How the AI Enhancer works, step by step

AEC Magazine's Enscape 4.1 report described the Enhancer as aimed at Enscape's in-house low-polygon people and vegetation, using "an AI engine that identifies which pixels should be enhanced." The Enscape documentation sets out the current routine. You open the Render window, tick Upload to Cloud and Start enhancement, which shows the credits required, pick a Chaos Cloud folder and render. The result lands in Chaos Cloud, where a dropdown switches between versions and the image can be shared by link, email, embed or QR code. The docs require a signed-in Chaos account "with a non-trial license." Chaos's Enhancer blog post says trial users receive 100 credits to test it, so check your licence before planning around a trial.

On Chaos's account, the AI detects people, vegetation and surfaces, lets you choose which to enhance, and for people lets you set age group, gender, ethnicity, hair colour and facial expression, with a creativity control and a side-by-side comparison. The November 2025 advanced controls, a public beta, added object masks, clothing style for people and separate refinement for trees, shrubs and grass. Chaos quotes 10 to 30 seconds per vegetation enhancement and 15 to 30 for people. Release notes for 4.18.1 macOS (27 May 2026) and 4.18.2 LTS Windows (24 June 2026) both say the feature "is no longer in beta." For offices with a policy on generative tools, the documentation notes that from Enscape 4.10 administrators can switch AI features off with the CHAOS_DISABLE_GEN_AI environment variable.

What do users say about its people?

The longest public record is a Chaos forum thread, "Share your Chaos AI Enhancer Renderings!" Early posts in July 2024 showed before-and-after comparisons users were pleased with. One user reported the same month that the "AI will put an adult face on a child." By November 2024 another called the output "not fit for purpose," and a third argued Enscape should put the effort into better assets and materials instead. These are community reports from the preview era, before the advanced controls and the end of the beta, so treat them as a checklist, not a verdict on today's build. An enhancer that ages toddlers is still a useful reminder to inspect every face before a render leaves the office.

How the AI Material Generator works

The 4.14.0 release notes put it inside the Material Editor: click Generate Material, upload a photo or texture into a Cosmos window, and Enscape returns "a complete material with all maps applied," which appears in the editor. The documentation adds the conditions: Enscape 4.14 or later, Chaos Cosmos running, a signed-in Chaos account and an internet connection, because generation happens in the cloud. Input tops out at 2K square. You get albedo, bump/normal and roughness maps, and you can use the result to replace an existing material in the project.

Chaos's launch post (19 December 2025) calls it an "early beta," says generation takes from seconds to about a minute depending on resolution, and promises more capabilities later. Later fixes were small: 4.17.0 fixed the tool's feedback button, and 4.21.0 fixed the wrong material being selected after an AI-generated import. In AEC Magazine's October 2025 feature, Chaos's Roderick Bates presented it as part of keeping AI steps inside one ecosystem ("Here, it's all part of an ecosystem") rather than downloading and re-uploading files between tools. For how photo-derived maps fail and how to check them, see ArchiGen's guide to AI PBR maps from photos.

Is there an Enscape AI tool for cutout people?

Not under that name. None of the Enscape release notes or documentation checked for this article describe an AI cutout-people feature; the AI that touches people is the Enhancer, working on Enscape's own 3D people. The nearest new tools sit in the asset library. Enscape 4.19.0 added Chaos Assistant features, including Asset Suggest, which recommends Cosmos assets from an image, a prompt or the viewport (its known issues list precision limits on placement), and Light Mood Match, since renamed Sky Match. Enscape 4.21.0 added the AI Assets Generator beta: "upload a reference photo and Enscape generates a textured 3D model," filed under Downloaded, 3D Models, AI-Generated.

How do these relate to Veras?

They do different jobs on the same view. Veras generates new images from your model, the Enhancer refines a render you have already made, and the Material Generator makes a material for the model itself. Veras has been inside Enscape since 4.7.0, which sends the rendered view and material ID layers, offers a Geometry Override Slider to set how closely the AI respects the CAD geometry, and can confine changes to part of the image. ArchiGen's Chaos Veras review covers its engines and versions.

The overlap is materials. Chaos's Veras what's-new page now advertises turning "any reference into a material," or matching it to a Cosmos material "with mapping, scale and alignment preserved." That is the same promise from the Veras side, and ArchiGen's scale and provenance audit sets out what to check before trusting either.

What it costs, in credits

Chaos moved its AI features onto one currency in May 2026. The Chaos Credits announcement lists monthly allowances of 100 credits on Solo, 500 on Premium and 1,500 on Collection, shared across Veras, the AI Upscaler, the AI Enhancer and cloud rendering, with top-ups in packs of 250, 1,000 or 3,500. Unused monthly allowance credits "do not roll over." The Enscape pricing page lists Solo at $47.90 a month per licence, billed annually at $574.80, with Veras AI included and an estimate of "Up to 5~ AI enhanced scenes" a month.

The arithmetic is short. A Solo allowance buys five full enhancements, or twenty 1k materials, or ten 1K Nano Banana 2 images in Veras, and every one comes out of the same pot. Architosh reported the move as Veras arriving in every Enscape, V-Ray and Corona product. Digital Production was drier, warning that credits make AI ideation "something you may want to track like any other billable resource." ArchiGen's pieces on the shared credit system and Chaos's 2026 product line follow that money further.

Who should use which

On Chaos's own framing, the Material Generator suits the surfaces nobody will scrutinise: the neighbour's render, the paving beyond the site line, the joinery in a background room. At 5 credits for a 1k material it is the cheapest operation in design8's credit table, and the input limit and beta label argue against using it for specified finishes. The Enhancer makes most sense on a few presentation views where the stock people and planting are the weakest thing in the frame, checked face by face. On Solo, a week of Veras exploration can quietly spend the month's enhancements.

Enscape's AI is best read as a set of meters attached to the last mile. Spend accordingly.
