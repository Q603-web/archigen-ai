Fenestra AI and Finch 3D: one paints the building, the other plans it.

Fenestra AI is a browser-based AI renderer for architects, priced from $8 a month billed yearly; Finch 3D generates multifamily floor plans, and its free plan edits but does not generate. Each is worth trying for that one job. This review reads both vendors' pricing and documentation plus independent coverage. ArchiGen has not run either tool.

What is Fenestra AI, and is Finch 3D free?

Fenestra AI (fenestra.app) is a browser-based AI renderer that turns sketches, drawings and 3D models into images and video, from $8 a month billed yearly. Finch 3D, now branded simply Finch, generates multifamily floor plans. Its Free plan costs €0 but excludes generation, which starts on Basic at €79 a month.

The two names have shared a page here since April, and the pairing has aged badly. The earlier version of this article described Fenestra as a facade and daylight optimiser and reported project results ArchiGen never produced. Neither part survives. Fenestra renders pictures. The facade-performance software that description comes closest to is FenestraPro, a separate company whose platform runs in Revit and Autodesk Forma. If you came for facade thermal, solar-gain and daylight analysis, you want that one.

What follows is a documentation review. ArchiGen has not used Finch or Fenestra on anything. Every claim below comes from a page opened on 10 October 2026, labelled by who said it.

Finch, formerly the "3D"

The product still lives at finch3d.com, but the homepage now calls it "Finch," with the tagline "AI for how the world builds" and an "AI-native platform for building design." The Rhino plug-in is now one door into a web app at app.finch3d.com. Øresund Startups, reporting a €1 million round in April 2025 led by Ampli Ventures, says the company was founded in 2020, followed a €2.5 million round led by Inventure in late 2022, and had a waiting list of over 80,000 architects. The homepage now claims a 130,000-plus enterprise waitlist and, in a stat block that has plainly lost its labels, "100,000+" countries of use. That would be a great many countries.

On Finch's own product page, the method is a firm's Plan Library: past plans, embedded with accessibility rules and local code, which Finch uses "as a base for every generation." An AI agent called Archie handles door placement, compliance checks and linked-unit updates. The Archie documentation is more specific and more modest: it queries GFA, NIA and unit counts, edits doors, windows and wall thicknesses, and exports schedules as CSV or XLSX. That is a drafting assistant with good manners, and the docs are honest enough to say so.

The scope is narrower than "how the world builds." Finch's About page says the generative tools work for multifamily residential only; massing tools work for all programs. Its typology table marks curved buildings as not supported by the AI floor plate, and angled roofs as unsupported throughout. The FAQ adds that AI unit plan generation is "currently only available for our strategic AI customers." Housing architects, then, and mainly rectilinear ones. For the wider case for agents in this kind of work, see our piece on agentic AI tools for architects.

How much does Finch cost, and is Finch 3D free?

Yes, with a large asterisk. The Finch pricing page lists three plans:

Finch planListed price (10 Oct 2026)What the vendor says it adds
Free€0, single userAll editing tools, real-time building performance metrics
Basic€79 billed monthly, 14-day free trialGenerate optimised unit mix and circulation
EnterpriseFrom €14,500 billed annually, 3 seatsAI floor plan generation, code and compliance check, custom BIM export, the AI agent, SSO

So the free plan is a capable editor with live area data and no generation at all. Basic is where Finch starts proposing unit mixes and corridors. Full floor plan generation and the agent sit on Enterprise, which works out to roughly €400 per seat per month at the three-seat minimum. Prices have moved: Sinan Ozen's Illustrarch review of April 2026, a source-based piece, not a hands-on test, listed Basic at €49 and Enterprise at €12,000 a year. Trust the vendor page, and check it again before you budget.

Where Finch plugs in

This is Finch's strongest suit on paper. The FAQ lists Rhino 8, 7.24+ and 6 SR35+, Grasshopper, Revit 23 to 27, and Autodesk Forma, with Archicad through Grasshopper; MicroStation and SketchUp are "not yet supported." The two big connections behave differently, and the docs say so plainly. With Rhino, the plug-in can auto-receive changes from the web app, so geometry on Finch's layers stays in sync. With Revit, the link is "uni-directional": you upload an empty conceptual mass, design the interior in Finch, then download a BIM model, and edits made in Revit cannot go back. The export page covers Revit families, 2D and 3D layers to Rhino, a Grasshopper stream, and PNG and CSV outputs.

That one-way Revit handoff is the detail a BIM manager should read twice. It is the same seam our BIM-first AI stack guide keeps finding: the AI layer upstream, documentation downstream, and no way to send a late change back up the hill.

Fenestra: a renderer with a model menu

Fenestra's homepage calls it "AI rendering software for architects and interior designers," turning sketches, CAD drawings, photos and 3D models into renders and animations "inside one browser-based studio." Its About page says Shaun McCallum, a Scottish architectural designer who worked at HASSELL, John Wardle Architects and Meta Reality Labs, founded it in London in 2024; it is now based in the UAE and claims over 20,000 architects and designers. The terms name the operator as Designtree AI LLC.

Inputs, per the vendor, are JPG, PNG and WEBP images, PDF drawings, and GLB, FBX or 3DM models. Rhino files import natively as 3DM; Revit and SketchUp models go in as exported FBX or GLB. There is no plugin, by design. The engine is a rotating cast of third-party models. The changelog for 1 September 2026 added a model browser and four video models, including Seedance 2.5; earlier entries add GPT Image 2 editing, Nano Banana 2 Lite and PDF import. The terms name Modal Labs, Replicate and Runway ML as processors.

The agent angle is new and genuinely useful. Fenestra publishes a connector for ChatGPT and Claude at mcp.fenestra.app: share a sketch or a Rhino view in the chat, ask for a render or a video, and the result lands in your Fenestra account, billed from the same credits. ChatGPT needs a paid plan with Developer mode on.

How does Fenestra price its credits?

The Fenestra pricing page sells credits: about 1 per image, 2 per enhancement and 10 per video. Individual is $8 a month billed yearly ($96) for 200 credits; Pro is $28 ($336) for 1,000; Studio is $48 ($576) for 2,000; Team is $2,500 a year ($208.33 a month) for 5,000 credits a month, shared across up to five seats; Enterprise is custom, and custom model training is an Enterprise service. The higher figures shown beside each price ($16, $56, $96) appear to be monthly billing, though the page labels the yearly saving "20%", which those numbers do not support.

The free tier is where Fenestra's own documents disagree. The pricing FAQ says new accounts get 5 credits and the free plan is non-commercial. The terms, last updated 24 October 2025, say the free tier gets 10 credits a month and grants Fenestra a licence to show free-tier outputs in its public gallery and marketing. AEC Hub's unsigned review, which does not describe any hands-on testing, flags further gaps between terms and pricing, no data processing agreement, and "57 third-party image and video models." Before client work, read the current terms yourself, and plan the trial with our render-credit evaluation guide.

They do not compete. Finch decides how many flats fit and where the corridor goes; Fenestra makes the resulting block look like somewhere you would rent. A housing practice might use both, months apart. A practice that never does repetitive residential has little reason to open Finch, and anyone needing geometry-faithful images should read our note on native integration versus file upload before trusting an uploaded view.

Finch is selling a planning department in a browser tab, priced like one. Fenestra is selling a very good paint box for the price of lunch. Judge each by the job it claims.
