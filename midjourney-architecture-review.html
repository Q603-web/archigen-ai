Midjourney for architecture: a mood machine, not a renderer.

Midjourney's current default model, V8.2, is a fast, stylish image generator that suits mood boards and early concept imagery. It cannot read a Revit, SketchUp or Rhino model, so it will not render your building. This review reads Midjourney's documentation, its update posts and independent coverage. ArchiGen has not run Midjourney on any project.

ArchiGen AI Editorial
7 min read · Rewritten October 2026 · Updated 28 October 2026 · Based on public sources

Is Midjourney any good for architecture?

For concept work, yes. Midjourney V8.2, the default since 24 July 2026, turns text and reference images into atmospheric pictures, which suits mood boards, material studies and early form ideas. It cannot open a Revit, SketchUp or Rhino model. To render a designed building, a model-aware tool such as Chaos Veras fits better.

The earlier version of this page reviewed a "V8.1" that it said arrived in February 2026, and it described studio routines and results ArchiGen never produced. Both are gone. What follows is a documentation review: Midjourney's help centre and update posts, Chaos's Veras pages, and press coverage, all checked on 28 October 2026. Where a sentence is a judgement rather than a vendor fact, it reads like one.

Which Midjourney version is current?

The V8 family arrived in stages. The Decoder reported on 18 March 2026 that V8 had opened for community testing on Midjourney's alpha site, with generation roughly five times faster and a new --hd mode rendering natively at 2K. Midjourney's Version page says V8.1 was released on 14 April 2026 and was the default from 10 June to 23 July. The V8.1 announcement promised images "in only 4 seconds in SD, and 12 seconds in HD," and HD output at "4x the resolution of V7 images."

V8.2 followed on 24 July 2026, pitched at "aesthetics and image quality and personalization," and it is the default today. Per the Version page, standard images start at 1024 by 1024 pixels; HD images are 2048 pixels and cost 1.3 minutes of GPU time against 0.8 for standard. On 27 August Midjourney opened testing of an Edit model for V8 that edits images by instruction, inpaints and outpaints, and takes up to four image references at once, replacing Omni Reference.

Where Midjourney earns a seat

The tools that matter to an architect are the ones that control mood. A Style Reference captures "colors, medium, textures, or lighting" from an image without copying its objects, which is exactly what a material or atmosphere board is for. Moodboards go wider, building a personal style from a set of chosen images. Midjourney's V8.1 post says style references, personalization and aesthetics stay consistent between V7 and V8.1, so a practice's existing references should carry over.

Speed changes the economics of looking. At the four seconds an image Midjourney quoted for V8.1, twenty variations on "a chapel on a moor in October light" cost less time than one coffee. Midjourney's video model adds five-second clips that start from an image you choose, enough for a mood loop on a pitch slide. Every one of those outputs is a new building, invented to match the words. That is the point of concept work, and the problem with everything after it.

Where it stops: your actual building

Midjourney works from text prompts and images. There is no Revit or SketchUp plug-in in its documentation, and its terms say it does not offer an API and prohibit automating the service. You can drop a model screenshot in as an image reference, and the Edit model will restyle it, but the software sees pixels, not walls, levels or window families. Nothing in the documentation promises that the window count you modelled is the window count you get back.

The Decoder adds a caution on prompts: V8 remains a pure diffusion model and, in its reading, trails competitors on complex, abstract instructions. For orthographic drawings, door schedules or anything a contractor will measure, use the model and the drafting tools. Our piece on geometry-aware AI renderers covers the tools built for that job.

Which is better for architects, Veras or Midjourney?

They answer different questions, and the documentation makes the split plain. Chaos describes Veras as working with Revit, SketchUp, Rhino, Archicad, Vectorworks, Forma and Allplan, plus a web app, and pitches it for exploring "façade treatments within your existing model geometry." Its page claims that with Nano Banana prompt control "only specified geometry and materials change, keeping your massing intact." That is a vendor claim, not an ArchiGen result, but it is the claim Midjourney never makes.

Which Midjourney plan does a practice need?

As listed on 28 October 2026 on Midjourney's plan comparison:

PlanMonthlyYearlyFast GPU timeNotable limits

Pro$60$576 ($48/mo)30 hrStealth mode, unlimited Relax video
Mega$120$1,152 ($96/mo)60 hrStealth mode, unlimited Relax video

Two rules decide the plan for most firms. Midjourney's commercial use page says a business grossing more than $1,000,000 a year needs Pro or Mega to use images commercially. And Stealth mode, which keeps images out of public view, exists only on Pro and Mega, which matters if you are generating imagery for an unpublished competition entry. Extra fast time is $4 an hour on any plan. A sole practitioner under the revenue line can start on Standard; a mid-sized practice is a Pro customer whether it likes it or not.

Use it to find the building. Then close the tab and draw it.
