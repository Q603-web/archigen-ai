MyArchitectAI: nine seconds and the small print

MyArchitectAI is worth it for architects and interior designers who want fast concept renders from SketchUp exports or sketches: $29 a month buys unlimited stills. Its speed and geometry claims are the vendor's own, and its free-tier terms give the company ownership of the renders you generate, plus a licence to use what you upload. ArchiGen has not run it.

Is MyArchitectAI worth it in 2026?

For fast concept images from a SketchUp export, screenshot or sketch, probably yes: paid plans start at $29 a month with unlimited still renders and a commercial licence. The speed and geometry claims are the vendor's own. Avoid the free tier for client work, because its terms give MyArchitectAI ownership of free-account designs.

The homepage of MyArchitectAI carries a stopwatch figure to one decimal place: "9.3s Average rendering time." Beside it sit "7,500,000+ Renders generated to date" and a third statistic, "0 Time to learn needed", a number only marketing could love. The headline promise is "Photorealistic AI rendering in under 10 seconds."

Nine point three seconds is a vendor's measurement of its own servers. ArchiGen has not run MyArchitectAI, so this review cannot confirm it, and an earlier version of this page that claimed otherwise has been withdrawn. What can be checked is everything around the stopwatch: what you upload, what you pay, who owns the result, and what paying users say on the record.

What it does, in the vendor's words

The workflow on the homepage has three steps. Upload "an interior or architectural design from SketchUp, Revit, Archicad, or any other CAD or 3D modeling software", click Render, download in 4K. The engine, MyArchitectAI says, works "while keeping the geometry and textures unchanged", and "Prompt is optional." Around that core sit four tools: style transfer from a reference image or preset, render editing that "will regenerate only the selected area", an enhancer for low-resolution renders with a "creativity slider", and animations built from a still with "30+ cinematic camera movements", ready "in 90-120 seconds" as an MP4.

The FAQ is more modest than the hero banner, and more interesting for it. "The main purpose of MyArchitectAI is not to create the competition-level visuals but to let you iterate through client feedback faster and win more work," it says, adding: "With 5% of the time and effort you can get 95% of the result of traditional 3D rendering software." The ratio is unaudited. The first sentence is a fair description of where image-to-image tools belong.

Does MyArchitectAI work with SketchUp?

Yes, through an image. The SketchUp page says you "Upload an image of your SketchUp interior or architectural design" and promises renders "from any SketchUp view in under 10 seconds." It also claims that rivals, "including SketchUp Diffusion", tend "to change your original design", while MyArchitectAI "preserves your SketchUp model's geometry and textures." The party making that claim is the one selling it.

There is no plugin yet. The homepage lists a SketchUp plugin and a Revit add-on as "Soon", and the vendor's public roadmap shows "SketchUp plugin v1" and "Revit plugin v1" as "In Progress", with Archicad, Rhino and 3ds Max plugins further back. So "SketchUp-friendly" means export a view, upload it, download the render. The accepted formats are JPEG, PNG and WebP, and the pricing FAQ supports "CAD exports (except for floor plans), line drawings, and hand sketches." The trade-offs of that round trip are the ones in our piece on native integration versus PNG upload: the model never sees your geometry, only a picture of it.

How much does MyArchitectAI cost?

This is where MyArchitectAI breaks from most of its category. There are no render credits on the current pricing page. Every paid plan lists "Unlimited renders", "Unlimited edits" and "Unlimited enhancer", and the meters are on animations, resolution and storage instead.

Prices are "in USD, per user, and exclusive of local taxes", and every paid plan includes a "Commercial usage license." One caveat sits in the FAQ: unlimited covers "all features related to rendering and editing static images", while "experimental tools" "sometimes come with usage limits." That changes the arithmetic in our cost per usable image analysis: when a failed render costs nothing extra, the price of a bad frame is your time, not your credits.

Read the refund promise twice. The pricing FAQ says MyArchitectAI will "fully refund you" if you "created fewer than 20 renders." The terms of service, effective 25 February 2026, are tighter: a request "within three (3) calendar days of your first payment", fewer than 20 images, and no images or animations downloaded in that period. Section 5.3.2 goes further and refuses refunds for "dissatisfaction with style, creative output, or subjective preferences once outputs have been generated", which sits awkwardly beside the FAQ's promise to refund "if for some reason our software doesn't meet your expectations".

Who owns a MyArchitectAI render?

On a paid plan, you do. The terms say that for Pro Accounts "you retain full ownership rights to any User Content and Designs" and "No license is granted to MyArchitectAI." On the free tier the same section reverses: "For Free Accounts, MyArchitectAI owns all rights to the Designs generated through use of the Services," and free users grant an "irrevocable, nonexclusive, royalty-free" worldwide licence to "reproduce, distribute, publicly display" their uploads and designs "for the purposes of providing, promoting, and improving the Services."

That sits awkwardly beside the marketing. The security page says "Whether you're a Free or Pro plan user, we never use your designs to train our AI models" and "You keep complete ownership of all renderings created using MyArchitectAI." The privacy policy, updated 22 September 2026, repeats the no-training pledge and extends it to the "AI processing providers", which it names as fal, Replicate and Runware in the US. Uploads are deleted within 24 hours of closing an account.

The training promise and the free-tier licence can be read together, but only because the privacy policy rules training out: the licence itself covers "providing, promoting, and improving the Services", wording broad enough to include training on its own. The ownership statements cannot both be true. Until the terms and the security page agree, treat the contract as the one that counts, and keep client drawings off a free account. Our guide to who owns an AI render covers why that matters when you invoice for the image. The terms also carry a binding arbitration clause with a 30-day opt-out.

What users report, labelled

The largest independent pool is Capterra, which listed 86 reviews on 10 October 2026, with sub-scores of 4.6 for ease of use and 4.4 for customer service, and notes it may earn referral fees. MyArchitectAI reposts some on its own reviews page. The praise is speed and ease; the complaints recur.

On geometry, a director at a small architecture practice wrote in October 2025 that "The program has difficulty recognising architectural elements which are not standard, like angled columns, windows which are not square or rectangular." On editing, another reviewer reported that each change made the image lose detail and look "less and less realistic"; another, otherwise glowing, said added objects "often do not import at the correct scale." An Italian designer, for anyone searching MyArchitectAI recensioni, noted it sometimes adds elements nobody asked for. Several late-2025 reviewers complained about buying credits for edits, which the current pricing page no longer lists. A July 2026 one-star review cited artificial renders and unanswered support.

On a SketchUcation thread started in September 2025, co-founder Kacper Staniul was candid: "We're not trying to replace physically-based renderers, as the level of precision of AI image models is still not there (at least for now)." A competitor's take, Rendershop's MyArchitectAI review, describes a $19 plan with 50 renders, watermarks and preset-only styling, none of which matches MyArchitectAI's current pages; read it as marketing, and see our Rendershop review for that tool on its own terms.

The verdict, for whom

MyArchitectAI has a clear idea of itself, and its founders say the honest part out loud. It is a fast, flat-priced concept tool for designers who already have a SketchUp or CAD view and want a client-friendly image of it today. The edit tools promise to swap a cladding or a worktop without going back to the model.

What the evidence does not support is the line "keeping the geometry and textures unchanged" as a guarantee. User reports describe odd columns, lost detail and stray objects, which is what any image model does on a bad day. Check every frame against your geometry hallucination checklist, and if the image has to prove the building rather than sell the mood, a model-linked plugin of the kind compared in our Veras versus Rendair piece is the safer tool.

Pay the $29, read section 6 of the terms, and time the render yourself. Nine seconds is the vendor's number; the checking is yours.
