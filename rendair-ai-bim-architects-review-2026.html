Rendair AI review: the BIM play, read closely

Rendair AI is worth trying for fast concept images, but it is not a BIM tool: there is no Revit or Archicad plugin, so you export a view and upload it. Plans run $19 to $190 a month, and subscription fees are non-refundable. ArchiGen has not tested it.

Is Rendair AI worth it for Revit and Archicad users?

For fast concept images, plausibly. Rendair AI is a browser tool with no Revit or Archicad plugin: you export or screenshot a view, upload it and prompt. Plans list at $19, $49 and $190 a month. Firms that want renders tied to the live model should compare Chaos Veras first. ArchiGen has not tested Rendair.

This is a documentation review. ArchiGen has not run Rendair on any model, so nothing here judges image quality. Vendor statements are labelled as the vendor's, and every source is listed at the end.

What Rendair says it is

The Rendair homepage lists five tools: Rendair Chat, which generates and edits renders through natural-language conversation; Create & Render, for turning drawings or 3D models into images; Edit & Adjust; Upscale & Enhance; and Video & Animate. The same page says the tool is "Used by 500,000+ architects worldwide" and, further down, invites visitors to "Join 300,000+ satisfied creators". Neither is independently checked, and they disagree.

The clearest description of the product is in Rendair's help article on how it fits a traditional visualization pipeline. Rendair says it "sits between your raw 3D model and the final image", replacing texturing, lighting and render setup. You screenshot a viewport, describe materials and mood in a prompt, then use in-painting to swap details and the upscaler for print. Standard downloads are about 1MP; the upscale tools offer 2K, 4K, 6K or 8K, and video comes out as MP4.

It is a pitch about atmosphere, by Rendair's own account: the same article says the AI "may hallucinate small details or misinterpret complex structural connections" and is "best for visualization, not construction documentation."

How does Rendair connect to Revit and Archicad?

By upload. Rendair's help centre states that it is entirely web-based, needs no installation, requires a constant internet connection and has no offline mode. Accepted files are JPG, PNG, WEBP, TIFF, HEIC, AVIF and single-page PDF, up to 10MB per upload. The pipeline article adds that screenshots are preferred, but "specific tools" accept OBJ, FBX, STL and IFC for object insertion; it does not say any tool renders a whole model from those files.

Rendair's AI Rendering for Revit page is honest in its own way. It promises to "turn Revit models into realistic photos without complex plugins" and to give "better visuals right from your exported views." The features target BIM habits: Materialize Views turns a "Shaded" Revit view into a photoreal image, Fix Plain Models says "Rendair guesses the right material for the object", Show Phases separates existing from new work, and Edit Renders lets you change materials or lighting with "no need to re-export from Revit." Note the word guesses: your Revit material assignments do not travel.

Archicad users get even less ceremony. The host-application pages linked from Rendair's homepage cover Rhino, Grasshopper, Vectorworks, Blender, AutoCAD and SketchUp. Neither BIM heavyweight is in that menu, and unlike Revit, Archicad has no page of its own among the sources checked here. Archicad does appear in Rendair's blog post Top AI Tools for BIM Architects in 2026, which ranks Rendair first among five tools and describes the workflow plainly: "Take a viewport screenshot from your Revit or Archicad model. Upload it to Rendair AI." The same post's comparison table lists Rendair's integration as "Image-based (Universal)". Universal is the polite word for no plugin, though it does mean the route works the same from anything that can export a JPG.

One Rendair page muddies this. The 3D Model to Render tool page tells users to "import your 3D model file from SketchUp, Revit, Rhino, Blender, or other 3D software", which sits awkwardly with the help centre, where screenshots are the preferred input and 3D files are mentioned only for object insertion. That conflict, and the plugin-versus-upload question in general, is covered in Chaos Veras vs Rendair AI and in the ArchiGen explainer on native BIM integration versus PNG upload.

What the AI knows about your model

Less than the marketing implies, and Rendair says so. Its help article on scale and proportions says the uploaded base image is treated "as the ground truth for geometry": the output keeps its aspect ratio, vanishing points and horizon line. Then comes the candid part. "The AI sees pixels, not measurements. It does not know a wall is 3000 mm long; it only knows it looks like a wall." It warns that small-scale line work may be misread, and points anyone needing "100% geometric accuracy" to Rendair's separate human-made rendering service.

The BIM blog post claims a "Creativity Strength" slider gives "precise control over how strictly the AI adheres to the original BIM geometry." That is the vendor's claim and ArchiGen has not checked it. For a Revit user the practical upshot is simple. Every design change means a fresh export and a fresh generation, and the image needs the kind of review set out in our geometry hallucination QA checklist before it goes near a client.

Paying yearly gets "2 Months for Free", and unused credits "never expire as long as your subscription stays active." The fine print lives elsewhere. Rendair's plans help article names four different tiers (Student, AI Creator, Pro, Team Pro), says images on lower tiers "may be selected for public display or promotional features", and says cancelling forfeits accumulated credits, though a plan can be paused for up to three months. It also says all paid images are "cleared for commercial use", while the pricing page lists a commercial license only on Pro and Studio Pro.

The payment policy, last updated 27 December 2025, is blunter: subscriptions renew automatically and "all subscription fees are non-refundable", including if you cancel mid-period. New users get 20 free credits, and the Revit page says no credit card is needed for them. Budget the rest using cost per usable image, not price per credit.

What do Rendair AI reviews say?

The independent picture is split. On Trustpilot, a community review site where reviewers are not verified as architects, Rendair held a TrustScore of 3.7 from 123 reviews on 10 October, with 67% five-star and 24% one-star. The five-star reviews keep praising the personal training sessions and support. The one-star reviews are mostly about billing: trouble cancelling, renewals reviewers did not expect, and prompts that miss and burn credits.

The aggregator Tooliverse (page verified 19 September 2026) gives a consensus of 8.71/10 from 219 reviews across five platforms. Its tally of watch-outs includes "Lacks native plugins for Revit and Archicad, requiring manual file exports" (15 reviews) and private mode being "restricted to higher-priced professional tiers" (12 reviews).

Read together, the reviews describe a product that is partly a service: training is written into the Pro and Studio Pro plans, and it is what happy customers mention most.

Rendair or Veras for BIM users?

The structural difference is plugin versus upload. Chaos lists Veras for Revit, Archicad and Vectorworks among other hosts, plus a web app, with a Starter plan at $16.90 a month billed annually for 500 credits. Chaos's own comparison article, written by a competitor, describes Rendair as having "no direct plugin for any host application." Our full side-by-side, with geometry controls and licensing, is in Chaos Veras vs Rendair AI: plugin vs image upload; the wider picture of which BIM platforms have native options is in native AI rendering by BIM platform.

Who Rendair suits

On the documentation, Rendair fits offices with mixed software, since an exported JPG looks the same to it whether it left Revit, Archicad or Vectorworks. It fits concept and early design stages, where a convincing mood matters more than a correct mullion, and people who would rather book a training call than learn a render engine. It fits less well where the same view is re-rendered weekly as the model changes, or where client work must stay private on a starter budget. The free credits are the right test: one of your own Revit views, judged by your own standard.

Rendair renders a picture of your model, not the model. Buy it knowing which one you need.
