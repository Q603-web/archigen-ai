SketchUp AI Render, and the plugins that still compete with it

SketchUp now ships its own AI renderer, AI Render, formerly Diffusion, paid for in credits included with every paid plan. Every third-party plugin must now answer one question: what does it do that the toolbar button does not? This guide reads Trimble's documentation and each vendor's pages. ArchiGen has not run any of these tools.

What is SketchUp AI Render?

AI Render is SketchUp's built-in generative renderer, formerly SketchUp Diffusion, renamed when Trimble launched SketchUp AI in December 2025. It needs a paid Go, Pro or Studio plan and costs 10 credits per image; those plans include 100 to 200 credits a month. Veras, Enscape, D5 and Spacely are the main plugin alternatives.

The earlier version of this page ranked seven plugins with star ratings and described six weeks of testing on a Philadelphia mixed-use scheme. That testing did not happen, and the ratings are gone with it. What replaces them is narrower and checkable: what each tool is, how it connects to SketchUp, and what it costs, from pages opened on 28 October 2026. The order below runs from the tool already inside SketchUp to the ones that only see an exported image.

SketchUp's own button: AI Render

Trimble's launch announcement of 10 December 2025 describes AI Render as "Trimble SketchUp's generative AI image creation tool," previously known as SketchUp Diffusion and run as a beta through SketchUp Labs. The relaunch added "Reference Images, Inpainting, and Negative Prompts." AEC Magazine's report of the launch confirms the rename. It ships alongside AI Assistant, which answers questions in the app and turns a text prompt or image into a 3D object through a tool called Generate Object.

The SketchUp 2026.1 release notes (released 9 December 2025) list what it does: generate photoreal images from the model viewport, set the visual style with a reference image, and mask parts of a result to erase, add or sketch elements. Trimble's SketchUp AI page says AI Render is built into SketchUp for iPad and Web, built into Desktop from version 26.1, and available as an extension for older supported Desktop versions. The getting-started guide sets the floor at SketchUp 2024, needs an internet connection, and warns that the model "works best when prompts are written in English."

What AI Render costs

Every generation, whether from a text prompt or the Sketch, Paint or Erase tools, costs 10 credits, according to the AI credits help page. At launch it cost 5, per AEC Magazine, so the price per image has doubled. Credits reset each month on your renewal date and do not carry over. Free SketchUp for Web users get the Help chat but not AI Render.

If you searched "SU84 AI render"

That is a different thing. SU84 Studio is a Vietnamese SketchUp training centre, and its software page lists a "SU84 Render AI" plugin. The page gives no price, version support or technical description, so there is nothing here to compare. Trimble's AI Render is the one documented by the company that makes SketchUp.

The plugins that sit inside SketchUp

Chaos Veras

Veras is the most direct rival, because it does the same job from the same place. Chaos lists SketchUp among its hosts alongside Revit, Rhino, Archicad, Vectorworks, Forma and Allplan, plus a web app, and says it offers "30+ models," naming Nano Banana, GPT Image and Veo 3.1 among them. As listed on 28 October 2026, Veras Starter is $16.90 a month billed annually ($202.80) for 500 Chaos AI credits a month, and Veras Pro is $29 a month billed annually ($348) for 2,000. Chaos's own conversion on that page puts Starter at about 50 Nano Banana 2 images a month. A 14-day trial is offered. For how it compares with an upload tool, see our Veras vs Rendair comparison.

Chaos Enscape

Enscape is a real-time renderer first and an AI tool second. It runs as a SketchUp extension, shows model changes live, renders stills up to 8192 by 8192 pixels and adds walkthroughs and VR. The AI comes through Chaos's shared credit pool. The Chaos pricing page lists Enscape Solo at $47.90 a month billed annually ($574.80), including Veras and 100 Chaos AI credits a month, which Chaos equates to about five AI-enhanced images or ten Nano Banana 2 images. If you want physically rendered stills and an AI pass in one subscription, this is the bundle that offers it.

D5 Render and D5 Lite

D5 now offers two routes from SketchUp, per its SketchUp page. D5 Lite is an "AI-native" plugin that runs inside SketchUp and saves materials and lighting into the .skp file. LiveSync streams the model into D5 Render, the full real-time renderer, which D5 says carries "10+ built-in AI features," including AI Atmosphere Match, AI Materials Snap and AI Enhancer. The D5 pricing page limits the free Community licence to non-commercial use with 50 trial AI credits; Pro lists AI use as unlimited. D5's own pricing guide puts Pro at $38 a month or $360 a year. We cover the AI features separately in D5 Render's AI features.

Spacely AI

Spacely offers a free extension from the SketchUp Extension Warehouse that renders interiors and exteriors and applies style transfer, with renders paid for in Spacely credits. Its pricing page gives a free account 40 trial credits and lists Personal, Studio and Agency plans; subscription prices did not display when we checked on 28 October 2026. Credit top-ups, for subscribers only, start at $9 for 200 credits. The plan lists lean toward interiors: Auto Furnish, Empty the Room, Collage to Render.

Lumion

Lumion's SketchUp story changed in September. The Lumion support note says Lumion View, its SketchUp plugin, was discontinued as a separate product on 15 September 2026 and "evolved into Axogram," a live architectural diagramming plugin; View subscribers get paid Axogram access. Rendering from SketchUp now means Lumion Pro, which the SketchUp page connects through a free LiveSync plugin, and which lists an AI upscaler and a material generator among its AI tools. As listed on 28 October 2026, Lumion Pro is $96 a month per named user, billed upfront at $1,149 a year.

The tools that only see a picture

Two names on most "SketchUp AI render" lists are not SketchUp plugins at all. They are browser apps that work from an image you export.

Rendair. Rendair's own SketchUp guide says it plainly: "Upload your SketchUp exports, get presentation-ready renders in minutes." As listed on its pricing page on 28 October 2026, Starter is $19 a month for 500 credits, Pro $49 for 1,500 credits and two members, and Studio Pro $199 for 7,500 credits and five members; yearly billing charges ten months for twelve. Unused credits roll over, which SketchUp's included credits do not.

MyArchitectAI. Its SketchUp page describes the same route: upload the design, click render, download. The pricing page lists Starter at $29 a month, Studio at $49 and Team at $99 for five seats, with unlimited still renders on every plan and 10 free renders to start, as listed on 28 October 2026.

Upload tools can be faster to start and easier to share. The trade is that the AI sees pixels rather than the model, so camera, materials and later edits live outside your file. Our note on native integration versus the PNG round trip sets out what that costs.

So which one, if you already pay for SketchUp?

Start with the button you own. If you hold a Go, Pro or Studio licence, AI Render is already installed or one Extension Warehouse download away, and your monthly credits will show within a dozen images whether AI concept renders earn a place in your week. If they do, the $11.99 add-on is the cheapest next step on this page.

Go elsewhere when AI Render cannot do the job. Veras covers the same ground with a wider choice of models and works in Revit and Rhino too, useful if SketchUp is not your only modeller. Enscape and D5 are for practices that need conventionally rendered stills, walkthroughs or VR, with AI as an extra. Spacely's feature list is written for interiors. The upload tools suit anyone who would rather keep rendering out of the model file altogether.

For the wider field beyond SketchUp, see our guide to AI architecture rendering tools. Trimble put a renderer in the toolbar. Every plugin now has to explain why you should not just press it.
