Snaptrude, actually the Revit alternative?

Snaptrude sells itself as AI-powered, browser-based BIM for early-stage design. This review reads its product pages, help docs, pricing, legal terms and independent coverage to set out what it does, how it exchanges with Revit, what it costs and who it suits. ArchiGen has not used it hands-on.

8 min read · Rewritten September 2026 · Based on public sources

This is a documentation review, not a hands-on test. ArchiGen has not used Snaptrude on a project. Everything below comes from Snaptrude's own product pages, help articles, pricing, release log and legal terms, plus independent coverage in AEC Magazine and Architosh. Where a statement is Snaptrude's own claim, it is labelled as such. The full list of sources is at the end.

The question in the headline is one Snaptrude itself invites: it publishes a "Snaptrude vs Revit" page and calls itself "The AI-Powered BIM software for Architects." The useful answer is narrower than the marketing. On Snaptrude's own account, it is built for early-stage design and hands off to Revit for detailed documentation.

What Snaptrude says it is

Snaptrude's homepage describes the product as a "design OS for AEC" that replaces "disconnected tools with one AI-native workspace for Site Analysis, Programming, Massing, BIM, and Presentation." It runs in the browser.

AEC Magazine's Martyn Day described the 2025 restructuring in "Snaptrude reimagined" (May 2025). The platform was split into four connected modes: Program, a structured, spreadsheet-like data view; Design, for conceptual massing and analysis; BIM, for walls, doors and windows; and Presentation/Documentation, for sheets and renders. Chief executive Altaf Ganihar told the magazine that "all these modes are connected, allowing data and views to be dragged and dropped between them."

The company is venture-backed. AEC Magazine reported in November 2023 that Snaptrude had raised $14 million in Series A funding from investors including Foundamental and Accel.

The AI features, as the vendor describes them

Snaptrude's homepage lists these AI features. All descriptions are vendor claims:

  • Program generation: "Generate detailed programs from prompts or uploaded RFPs and sites."
  • Auto-adjacencies: "One‑click adjacencies from codes and your program."
  • Mass to BIM: "Auto-convert mass to BIM", producing "walls, floors, roofs, and more, all in a single click."
  • AI renders: renderings of "different building views."
  • App Builder: "Describe a workflow, get an app."

The public release log shows how actively this layer is changing. Entries in 2026 include "Adjacency Diagrams in AI Panel" (29 January), "Knowledge" (10 February), "Update Agents" (13 February) and "New AI Pipeline and Improved Site Analysis" (12 March). The most recent entry when this article was written was release 3.53.1, dated 14 May 2026.

In an October 2025 interview with AEC Magazine ("Snaptrude on AI"), Ganihar said firms can connect their own document stores, naming "Google Drive or SharePoint or Egnyte or ACC", so the AI can draw on past project files. The article is interview-led and reports these as the company's statements, not as tested results. Snaptrude's student-plan announcement mentions complimentary AI credits and "discounted AI credits top-ups", which suggests AI use is metered by credits. The main pricing page does not list credit allowances, so check this before you buy.

Independent coverage gives a useful earlier baseline. In September 2024, AEC Magazine ("Snaptrude advances") described "physics-enabled AI" that stacks program blocks "based on adjacencies, into a building footprint." It singled out space planning as a particular strength.

How it exchanges with Revit

Revit into Snaptrude

There are two documented routes. Release 3.51.0, "Direct Revit Import" (10 April 2026), lets you drag and drop a Revit file into the New Project dialog, up to 150 MB. Snaptrude says processing takes 3-5 minutes. Larger files need the Snaptrude Manager plugin for Revit. The release notes add an important condition: "Revit files without rooms are not imported. AI queries in Snaptrude rely on room data to function." Room-less files can still go through Snaptrude Manager for space planning.

The Snaptrude Manager route is described in the help article "Upload & Edit a Revit Model on Snaptrude." You set a section box in a Revit 3D view, and "only those elements within the section box will be imported to Snaptrude." The article's mapping table shows that the translation is not one-to-one:

  • Walls, doors, windows, columns and ceilings convert directly. So do floors under 25mm thick.
  • Floors thicker than 25mm become a Snaptrude "Slab."
  • Casework, lighting fixtures, mechanical equipment and plumbing fixtures become "Furniture."
  • Beams, stairs, roofs, structural framing, topography and MEP components become "Mass."

The same article notes that "each time a Revit model is uploaded to Snaptrude, a new project will be created automatically." Re-uploading into an existing project is not supported, and materials must be applied correctly in Revit to display properly.

Snaptrude back into Revit

The help article "Import Snaptrude model to Revit" (updated 15 April 2026) describes exporting a ".trude" file from Snaptrude. You then use Snaptrude Manager's "Import to Revit" inside Revit, either to reconcile changes into the original Revit project or to start a new one. The listed transfers are:

  • Walls, floors, slabs, columns, doors and windows, as their respective Revit families.
  • Several stair types, as cast-in-place stairs "with editable parameters."
  • Floor labels, as room tags (when importing with labels enabled).
  • Custom tag values, as Revit room parameters.

One documented catch is that tags left at "Default" export empty.

Snaptrude's Revit comparison page goes further than the help docs: "Materials and custom parameters carry across." Treat that as a vendor claim until you have checked it on your own families and templates.

Other formats

According to the same comparison page (vendor claims):

  • IFC: "Native IFC 2x3 and IFC 4. No plugin."
  • Export: DWG, Rhino and IFC.
  • Import: Rhino/Grasshopper and SketchUp, plus Archicad through an add-on.

The firms page describes the integration as "Bi-directional Revit and Rhino."

Independent coverage adds context. Architosh's December 2023 analysis reported that "Snaptrude can round-trip with Revit." It also argued that the platform's larger vision needs "more data fluidity than file-based imports and exports." In practice, that means a sync you run, not a live link.

Documentation: where Revit stays

This is where the vendor's and the independent sources agree most clearly. Snaptrude's own Revit comparison page lists "Deep detailing, schedules, and construction documentation" and "Mature family and component libraries for delivery-grade BIM" as Revit territory, and calls deep documentation "Not the tool's job." AEC Magazine wrote in September 2024 that Snaptrude was "not as an end-to-end solution. For now, you would have to go back into Revit for coordination and documentation using the bidirectional link."

Snaptrude does produce drawings. Its July 2025 blog post describes floor plans, elevations, sections and site plans as "dynamic views of a single, unified model." AEC Magazine's 2024 piece mentions labelled drawings, sheet generation and PDF export. The release log added "Dimensions in Present Mode" in March 2026. None of these sources claims a permit-ready construction set. Snaptrude's own comparison page says the opposite.

Pricing and plans

Snaptrude's pricing page, accessed 30 September 2026, lists four tiers:

Plan Listed price Key inclusions (vendor list)
Free $0 3 free projects; basic BIM & Revit export; single user; limited presentation outputs; no teams environment
Individual $60/month Unlimited projects; basic BIM & Revit export; single user; unlimited presentation outputs; no teams environment
Organization $100/month, billed annually Teams & collaboration; licence/team management portal; 3 license transfers a year; custom roles & permissions; BIM library & custom RFAs
Enterprise Custom, billed annually Unlimited licences; SSO + SAML + MFA; open API access; premium support; directory sync

Two caveats. First, the pricing page does not say whether these prices are per user. Second, Snaptrude's own "vs Revit" page gives different figures, quoting "Professional $499 (BIM, IFC, .rvt, AI all included). Organization $1,199" under a 2026 pricing heading. The two pages use different plan names and prices, so ask for a written quote before budgeting.

Students and educators can get a free plan. Snaptrude's announcement says it covers students "currently enrolled in accredited architecture, design, or engineering programs", plus educators and institutions. It comes with a "1-year renewable license" and needs a school email or proof of enrollment.

The collaboration model

Real-time co-editing is Snaptrude's longest-standing pitch. Its v0.4.0 release post from December 2021 said architects "can, for the first time ever, design simultaneously on the same model, on the web." The current homepage promotes real-time collaboration with "teammates, consultants or clients."

The access model matters more than the pitch. The help article on sharing (updated 2 February 2026) says invited people get "default 'Viewer' permission." To give someone editing rights, you add them to a team in an organization. The "Anyone with the link" setting gives view-only access, and the alternative is "Restricted: Only those added." Because the Free and Individual plans list "No teams environment", shared editing in practice means the Organization tier or above.

Data and confidentiality

For a cloud tool holding client models, the legal pages matter as much as the feature list. Here is what they say.

Vendor security claims (firms page). The firms page says Snaptrude is "SOC 2 compliant with continuous monitoring and strict access controls" and that data is "Encrypted in transit and at rest including version history." It adds that "Ring-fenced AI queries stay in your IP," and that "Your project data is never shared or used to train models." Enterprise teams get "data residency options." These are marketing claims. If they matter to a contract, ask for the SOC 2 report and put the no-training commitment in writing.

Terms & Conditions (updated 18 February 2026). "You own User Content." You grant Snaptrude a "limited, non-exclusive, sub-licensable, royalty-free, and worldwide licence to access and use the User Content solely for the provision of the services." Usage data may be used in de-identified or aggregated form to improve the platform. On expiry or termination, "You will no longer have access to User Content", so export your models before a subscription lapses. The terms are governed by Delaware law.

Privacy policy (updated 25 February 2026). Processing happens primarily in the USA but may involve other jurisdictions, including India. Data is retained "for a reasonable period after termination."

Data Processing Agreement (effective 27 July 2026). Snaptrude acts as processor and the customer as controller. The listed subprocessors include Amazon Web Services and MongoDB for hosting, several analytics providers, and Anthropic PBC as "AI Model Provider (LLM / Claude API)." Snaptrude commits to notify customers "within 48 (Forty Eight) hours" of a personal data breach. It encrypts data at rest with "AES‑256 or equivalent" and in transit with "TLS 1.2+." On request at termination, it will delete data "to the extent technically possible."

If client NDAs restrict which third parties can process project data, the subprocessor list is the document to send to the client or your counsel. It names the third-party AI model provider.

Who it fits, on the evidence

Snaptrude's positioning and the independent coverage point the same way:

  • Teams that do concept, programming and massing work in Revit today and find it slow. Architosh framed Snaptrude as filling the early-design gap, noting that "Revit users hate Revit for conceptual design." AEC Magazine called it "especially useful in space planning."
  • Firms that stay on Revit for delivery. The documented round trip (direct import, then .trude export through Snaptrude Manager) fits a front-end role. The vendor's own comparison page assumes Revit for construction documents.
  • Students and educators, given the free student plan.

It is a weaker fit for:

  • Firms that want one tool through construction documents. Neither Snaptrude nor the independent coverage claims that.
  • Projects whose Revit models lack rooms, if you want to use the AI features. The direct-import release notes say those files are not imported.
  • Teams bound by client confidentiality terms that have not reviewed the DPA's subprocessor list and the AI no-training claim.

Day's broader caution in "Snaptrude reimagined" still applies. He reported that architects find the new cloud BIM tools "simply not comparable to mature desktop BIM tools," even though early-stage design in Revit "has never really been ideal."

What this review cannot tell you

Because ArchiGen has not run Snaptrude, this article cannot speak to modelling speed, the quality of the AI-generated layouts, how faithful the Revit round trip is on your templates, or performance on large models. The free plan (3 projects) is the obvious place to check these yourself. Before you commit, import a real Revit model that has rooms, export it back through Snaptrude Manager, and compare families, parameters and room data against the original.
