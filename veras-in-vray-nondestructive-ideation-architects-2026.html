Veras is inside V-Ray. Keep the scene in charge.

Veras inside V-Ray gives architects a fast image-ideation layer without rewriting the 3D scene. Use it to test atmosphere, material direction and client options. Return to V-Ray for geometry, measured light, repeatable cameras and final pixels.

Nora Sinclair
Nora Sinclair is ArchiGen's AI editorial persona, not a human architect or practitioner. Every piece is reviewed and approved by ArchiGen's human publisher. Full disclosure

What does Veras inside V-Ray change for architects?

Veras adds rapid AI image ideation inside V-Ray while leaving the underlying 3D scene untouched. That makes it useful for testing mood, materials and composition early. It does not turn generated pixels into editable V-Ray geometry, so the scene must remain the authority for design decisions and final production.

A V-Ray viewport can now take a short detour through generative AI without sending the model along for the ride. That separation is the feature.

Chaos says Veras is now available in V-Ray plans for SketchUp, Rhino and 3ds Max. A user can send a viewport screenshot or Virtual Frame Buffer image to Veras, generate styled options with Nano Banana 2, and bring the chosen direction back into the design conversation. The company is explicit about the boundary: Veras does not modify the 3D scene or its geometry.

Good. A generated image should be free to speculate. A project model should be difficult to accidentally rewrite.

Why is non-destructive AI the useful part?

The phrase “inside V-Ray” invites an assumption that the generator has become part of the renderer. It has not. Veras receives an image from the V-Ray context and returns another image. Materials, lights, assets and geometry in the scene remain where they were.

That makes the integration a branch for visual thought. It can suggest a warmer stone, denser planting or a different evening character while the authored scene remains available for measured correction. The arrangement also avoids a familiar production horror: a persuasive experiment quietly becoming the only surviving version of the work.

This is a cleaner division than pretending generated pixels are BIM intelligence. Our guide to what BIM integration actually means makes the same distinction. Proximity to a model does not prove that an AI output understands objects, parameters or specifications.

Where should Veras enter a V-Ray workflow?

Start after the camera and broad massing are credible, but before the team has spent hours perfecting every material. A weak camera fed to an image generator returns an expensively decorated weak camera. A stable view gives the generated options a fair job.

Send a clean viewport or VFB image with enough information to preserve the important relationships. Use Veras for a narrow question: facade warmth, planting density, weather, interior glow or entourage character. One question per branch makes the response legible.

Then select a direction and rebuild the consequential choices in V-Ray. If the generated image proposes limestone, specify a real material. If it proposes a line of trees, place assets at defensible intervals. If it invents a dramatic skylight, do not congratulate the lighting setup. Check whether the building owns a skylight.

Does Nano Banana 2 preserve the architecture?

Chaos describes Veras as using Nano Banana 2 and says its geometry controls let users decide how much of the original form is retained. Official Veras documentation lists two current Engine 7 routes: a faster Nano Banana 2 option and a higher-cost Nano Banana Pro option. It also lists older SD engines with numeric geometry and material controls.

Those are vendor claims and product settings, not proof that every output preserves a design. ArchiGen has not run a controlled test of the V-Ray integration. Generated images still need a comparison against the source frame. Facade bays, slab edges, openings, rails and site boundaries are the places where a lovely option can acquire fictional architecture.

The useful test is a difference review, not a vibe. Overlay the generated frame on its source at matched dimensions. Toggle between them and mark changes as intended, acceptable or rejected. The geometry-hallucination checklist provides a fuller inspection order.

How should client options be labelled?

A Veras frame can look more resolved than the decision behind it. That is especially risky in a client meeting, where polished glass and perfect planting may read as approved design rather than provisional atmosphere.

Label each generated option with its purpose and status. “Material mood study, generated from V-Ray viewport, geometry not verified” is not glamorous. It is accurate. Keep the source frame beside the option so everyone can see what the system changed.

Do not use generated alternatives to smuggle unpriced scope into the room. A new canopy, deeper reveal or mature grove may be visually persuasive and contractually imaginary. Mark such additions as proposals before they become memories.

This is where the integration can improve conversation. Instead of waiting for three fully authored scenes, a team can compare three bounded directions and commit production effort after one is chosen. The saving comes from postponing detail, not abolishing it.

What must return to V-Ray?

Anything that needs to survive another camera, a revision or technical scrutiny belongs back in the scene. That includes geometry, material scale, light positions, sun settings, asset placement and camera data.

A generated facade texture may be a persuasive cue, but it is not a mapped material. A glowing lobby may suggest the right hierarchy, but it does not establish fixture layout or exposure. The image is a brief for production. Treating it as production is how the second view exposes the trick.

For multi-view work, build the accepted direction once in V-Ray and render the camera set from that common scene. Our multi-angle consistency test explains why separate image generations can quietly produce separate buildings.

A practical handoff record

Save the source VFB or viewport capture, the Veras output, the prompt, the engine name, generation date and a short acceptance note. Record which visual ideas will be rebuilt and which generated details must be ignored. The file set does not need ceremony. It needs enough evidence for another person to understand the decision next Tuesday.

If several options are shown, keep the rejected ones and the reason for rejection. This prevents the mossy version from returning two weeks later under a new filename. It also turns a fast experiment into a traceable design choice.

The editorial position is simple: Veras belongs beside V-Ray, not on top of it. Let the generator make quick visual propositions. Let the renderer and its scene carry the obligations. Speed is useful when authority stays put.
