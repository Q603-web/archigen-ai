Archsynth and xFigura, read before you pay.

Archsynth suits solo designers who want cheap, high-volume sketch-to-render stills; xFigura suits teams who want many AI models on one shared canvas, with Revit and Rhino plugins. Roundups list them side by side, but they are different products. This review reads each vendor's pricing, docs and terms, plus user reports. ArchiGen has tested neither.

Is Archsynth or xFigura worth it?

Archsynth is worth a look for cheap, high-volume sketch-to-render stills on your own: Premium lists 3,000 credits a month for $590 a year. xFigura suits teams who want many AI models on one shared canvas, with Revit and Rhino plugins, from $16 a month. ArchiGen has not tested either.

The two names share a page in Chaos's roundup of AI architectural rendering tools, last updated 18 June 2026, which compares six options: Veras, Midjourney, Rendair AI, Archsynth, xFigura and Artlist. Chaos makes Veras, so that list is a competitor's map, not a referee's card. ArchiGen has already looked at how Chaos grades its own tool in that piece.

This is a documentation review. ArchiGen has not run Archsynth or xFigura. An earlier version of this page described a test on a mixed-use scheme that never took place, and nothing from it survives here. Everything below comes from each vendor's own pricing, help and legal pages, with independent and community sources labelled.

What is Archsynth, and who runs it?

On its homepage, Archsynth calls itself "The Most Comprehensive AI solution for Architecture and Interior design." The feature list is long: sketch to render, image to 3D, image to CAD, text to image, inpainting, upscaling, tileable textures, rendering videos and an image-reading assistant. The terms and conditions name the operator as GL Technologies, India.

The scale claims deserve a squint. The homepage says the tool is "Trusted by 176,582 Users" and, a few scrolls later, "Trusted by 18,415+ Users," alongside "15,698,726+ Renders already created." On integration, Archsynth says its image-to-3D models can be used "easily in Sketchup, Rhino, Revit, 3ds max and more," which describes an export, not a live plugin. Chaos's roundup agrees: no direct BIM plugin, so you upload images rather than work from the model.

How much does Archsynth actually cost?

The official pricing page sits behind a sign-up prompt but still lists four plans. Each shows a headline monthly figure that assumes annual billing, next to a "Normally" monthly price. Pro is $49 a month, or $490 a year (about $41 a month), for 1,000 credits. Premium is $79 a month, or $590 a year (about $49), for 3,000 credits, which the page prices at $0.016 per generation. Business is $199 a month, or $1,999 a year, for 10,000 credits at $0.017. Yes, the top tier costs more per image than the one below it. The Student plan ($19 a month, 200 credits) shows as "Student Plan Unavailable." Every tier lists a commercial usage licence, no watermark and no daily limits.

Two traps for the price-searcher. The homepage still runs a comparison table pricing Archsynth Student at $10 a month and Pro at $24, figures that do not match the pricing page. And the site at archsynth.net, which looks like the product's own site, is an affiliate page run by "the archsynth.net team" that says it "may earn a commission" on sign-ups. Its prices match the official ones as of its stated 11 August 2026 check. If you are comparing Archsynth's per-credit maths against other tools, ArchiGen's piece on cost per usable image explains why the sticker rate flatters every vendor.

What do Archsynth reviews say?

The largest independent pool is Archsynth's Trustpilot page, which showed a TrustScore of 2.8 out of 5 from 32 reviews when checked, on a profile the company has not claimed. The split is stark: 41% one-star, 31% five-star. Fans praise speed, realism and ease. The complaints are mostly about the business rather than the pixels: emails that go unanswered (reviews from July and August 2026), duplicate charges and trouble getting proper invoices (May and September 2026), and renewals continuing after cancellation requests. One December 2025 reviewer says the tool "disregards clearly written prompts" on precise interior requests. Thirty-two self-selected reviews prove little, but a billing cluster is what a practice manager will ask about. Archsynth's own reviews page carries three testimonials, two credited only to an "Architect" in Prague or Santa Monica. A search for Reddit discussion of either tool on 10 October 2026 turned up nothing useful.

What is xFigura?

xFigura's homepage sells an "AI Canvas for Architects": an infinite, shared board where you sketch, prompt and compare outputs from more than 90 third-party models. Its pricing FAQ names image models such as Nano Banana Pro, GPT Image 2 and Flux Klein, video models such as Kling and Seedance, and 3D models such as Tripo, Rodin, Meshy and Hunyuan3D. ETH Zurich's Digital Building Technologies group lists George Guida as xFigura's founder and CEO. A promotional April 2025 piece in Parametric Architecture, tied to a PAACADEMY launch offer and workshop, describes him as an architect and educator whose work spans Foster + Partners and Harvard.

The integration story is better than Chaos says and messier than the homepage admits. Chaos calls it Rhino-only. xFigura's docs now describe a Rhino plugin, installed from the Package Manager, that captures the viewport and imports meshes, and a Revit plugin on the Autodesk Marketplace whose headline feature is Batch View Import. Meanwhile the homepage and pricing FAQ still promote a Speckle integration that the docs mark as "currently unavailable," with the node removed from the canvas.

The social proof is heavy: a Patrik Schumacher quote, customer stories on Zaha Hadid Architects, BIG and KPF dated 14 July 2026, and a claim of "~500k+ Generations per month across top architecture firms." All are xFigura's own. AEC Magazine's AI Spotlight Directory lists the tool, but as a directory entry, not a review.

How much does xFigura cost?

The pricing page lists a Free plan with 25 credits, three canvases and non-commercial use only. Starter is $16 a month for 400 credits, "up to 400 images, 130 videos or 100 3D models," with ten canvases. Professional is $48 a month for 1,200 credits, unlimited canvases, and private images and boards. Team (shared credit pool, up to 30 users) and Enterprise (SSO/SAML, custom limits) are custom-priced. The page advertises 20% off annual billing, and Chaos reads the $16 figure as the annual rate, so check the toggle at checkout. Credit cost varies by model, and unused self-serve credits do not roll over.

One line needs clearing up before client work. The Starter card says "Personal-use license," yet the FAQ says commercial use is allowed "on any paid plan" and that the only restriction applies to Free. ArchiGen's guide on who owns an AI render covers why that wording matters; get the answer in writing.

Does either tool train on your drawings?

xFigura's homepage says "No model retraining, ever." Its own FAQ is more careful: on self-serve plans, the terms "permit xFigura to use customer content to maintain, improve, and train our products and services," while Enterprise agreements bar training on your visual content. Archsynth's terms take a different line: if you post with public privacy settings, you grant ArchSynth a worldwide, sublicensable licence to your prompts and outputs; private work stays yours. For confidential schemes, both point the same way, and ArchiGen's note on cloud rendering and client confidentiality sets out what to check.

Who each one suits

Archsynth suits a sole designer or small office that wants many presentation stills cheaply and is curious about image-to-3D and image-to-CAD. Premium annual is the cheapest per-credit rate either vendor lists. The risk is the company, not the canvas: given the Trustpilot billing complaints, start monthly, use the seven-day refund window, and keep your receipts.

xFigura suits a team that already works in Rhino or Revit, wants to try several image and video models without juggling subscriptions, and needs to share boards with colleagues or clients. Professional is the realistic floor for paid work because of private boards, though its licence is no clearer on paper: the comparison table marks Free and Starter "Personal", leaves Professional blank, and lists "Royalty-free" only for Enterprise. Firms with confidentiality clauses belong on Enterprise terms. If the render has to stay faithful to a documented model, neither is pitched as that tool; see ArchiGen's guide to geometry-aware renderers. Before paying, a plan for spending trial credits beats a free afternoon of prompting.

One sells renders by the thousand. The other sells a room to make them in. Buy the one that matches your problem.
